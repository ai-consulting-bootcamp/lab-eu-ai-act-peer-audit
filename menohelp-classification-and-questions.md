## Phase 2 classification table

| Question | Auditor's answer |
|---|---|
| Does this system fall under any prohibited category (Article 5)? | No prohibited practice is identified in the reviewed scope. Self-reported symptoms are not biometric emotion recognition. Supporting people experiencing symptoms is not, by itself, exploitation meeting Article 5's conditions. No social scoring, policing, facial scraping, biometric identification or image generation is documented. This is a scoped finding, not proof against every possible misuse. |
| Does this system operate in any of the eight Annex III areas? | No listed use is established. The eight areas are biometrics; critical infrastructure; education; employment; essential services; law enforcement; migration/borders; and justice/democracy. Health-related content alone does not satisfy point 5. No public-benefit eligibility decision (5(a)), credit decision (5(b)), insurance pricing (5(c)), or emergency dispatch/patient triage (5(d)) is documented. The code's `triage` node chooses report context; it does not allocate emergency care. |
| If Annex III: significant influence or narrow/preparatory task? | Not applicable on current evidence. Do not use Article 6(3)'s narrow-task exception to bypass an unassessed medical-device route. If a future Annex III use appears, assess material influence and profiling within that listed use. Personalisation outside Annex III does not automatically become high-risk. |
| Does it interact with users or generate content requiring disclosure (Article 50)? | Yes. Assess first-interaction disclosure under 50(1) and (5), and machine-readable marking of generated text under 50(2), subject to its conditions and exceptions. Private personal reports do not by themselves trigger the public-interest publication limb of 50(4). No deepfakes or biometric categorisation are documented. |
| First-pass risk tier | **Limited risk / transparency, provisionally for the stated wellness and educational purpose.** Medical-device qualification is unresolved and must be reviewed before an external pilot. This is not an unconditional low-risk finding. |
| One-sentence justification citing a specific provision | Direct user interaction and generated reports engage Article 50, while Article 6(1) with Annex I Section A points 11 (MDR) or 12 (IVDR) could make the product high-risk if it qualifies as a regulated device requiring third-party conformity assessment. |

The stated purpose supports the provisional result, but the actual personalised interpretation of blood values, possible causes and tests to discuss creates a material boundary question. A medical-device specialist must assess intended purpose, promotional claims, functionality and expected reliance together. If the medical route is confirmed, determine whether MDR or IVDR applies, the relevant classification rules and whether third-party assessment is required; do not assume all health applications or all medical software are automatically high-risk. For MDR software, Rule 11 is a specific escalation point. No final device class is assigned here. See legal sources L1-L5.
