# System brief - AI Search Visibility Agent (Barsha Mishra, Project 3)


**What the system does**
The AI Search Visibility Agent shows how visible a brand is online compared with its competitors. It checks two places: Google search results, and the answers an AI assistant gives to typical shopping questions (for example "best online shop for sneakers"). It then sends a written report by email. The report includes a visibility score per brand, tables, a chart, and recommended actions.

**Inputs**
A user fills in a web form (n8n form) with:
- their brand name and up to 4 competitor brand names
- a country
- up to 6 search questions
- the email address the report should be sent to

The system then collects:
- Google search results for each question (from Serper)
- AI assistant answers to the same questions (from the OpenAI API)
- recent news articles about the brands (from NewsAPI)
- passages from official SEO and AI-search guidelines, such as Google's documentation, stored in a search database (Pinecone)

**Personal data:** the main subjects are companies, not people. Some personal data is still involved: the recipient's email address, and possibly names of journalists, authors or company spokespeople that appear in news articles and search snippets.

**Outputs**
- A visibility score per brand: Google %, AI %, and overall. These are calculated in code, not by the AI.
- A written report generated by OpenAI from the collected data, including comparisons, "hidden competitors" (brands the AI mentions that the user did not list), and recommendations.
- The report as a Markdown file attached to a designed HTML email.

The system **recommends**. It does not take any action or decision on its own.

**Who is affected**
- **Directly:** the SEO/GEO managers and marketing leads who read the report and decide what to do.
- **Indirectly:** the competitor brands described in the report, and any people named in news articles.

No consumers, employees or job applicants are assessed.

**Human review**
No person checks the report before it is sent. Instead, an automated fact-checker (a LangGraph agent that also uses OpenAI) compares every claim in the text with the calculated data and fixes errors, in at most 2 rounds. The human review happens **after delivery**: the marketing lead reads the report and decides whether and how to act on it.

**Who built it**
I built it alone. It is a custom n8n workflow with a LangGraph fact-checker hosted on Render. It connects third-party services: OpenAI (language model), Serper, NewsAPI, Pinecone, Gmail (SMTP) and QuickChart.

**Who would use it in production**
SEO/GEO managers and marketing leads at online brands, or an SEO agency running it for its clients. They would use the reports to plan content and SEO work.
