# System brief - AI Search Visibility Agent (Barsha Mishra, Project 3)


**What the system does**
The AI Search Visibility Agent shows how visible a brand is online compared with its competitors. It checks two places: Google search results, and the answers an AI assistant gives to typical shopping questions (for example "best online shop for sneakers"). It then sends a written report by email. The report includes a visibility score per brand, tables, a chart, and recommended actions.

**Inputs**
A user fills in a web form (n8n form) with:
- their brand name and up to 4 competitor brand names
- a country
- up to 6 search questions
- the email address the report should be sent to

The system then collects:
- Google search results for each question (from Serper)
- AI assistant answers to the same questions (from the OpenAI API)
- recent news articles about the brands (from NewsAPI)
- passages from official SEO and AI-search guidelines, such as Google's documentation, stored in a search database (Pinecone)

**Personal data:** the main subjects are companies, not people. Some personal data is still involved: the recipient's email address, and possibly names of journalists, authors or company spokespeople that appear in news articles and search snippets.

**Outputs**
- A visibility score per brand: Google %, AI %, and overall. These are calculated in code, not by the AI.
- A written report generated by OpenAI from the collected data, including comparisons, "hidden competitors" (brands the AI mentions that the user did not list), and recommendations.
- The report as a Markdown file attached to a designed HTML email.

The system **recommends**. It does not take any action or decision on its own.

**Who is affected**
- **Directly:** the SEO/GEO managers and marketing leads who read the report and decide what to do.
- **Indirectly:** the competitor brands described in the report, and any people named in news articles.

No consumers, employees or job applicants are assessed.

**Human review**
No person checks the report before it is sent. Instead, an automated fact-checker (a LangGraph agent that also uses OpenAI) compares every claim in the text with the calculated data and fixes errors, in at most 2 rounds. The human review happens **after delivery**: the marketing lead reads the report and decides whether and how to act on it.

**Who built it**
I built it alone. It is a custom n8n workflow with a LangGraph fact-checker hosted on Render. It connects third-party services: OpenAI (language model), Serper, NewsAPI, Pinecone, Gmail (SMTP) and QuickChart.

**Who would use it in production**
SEO/GEO managers and marketing leads at online brands, or an SEO agency running it for its clients. They would use the reports to plan content and SEO work.

# Phase 4 — Audit Report

## 1. System summary
The agent compares a brand's visibility with competitors across Google search results and AI-generated answers. A user submits brands, a country, search questions and a recipient email through an n8n form. The workflow combines search and news data with retrieved SEO guidance, calculates visibility scores in code, and emails an AI-written report with recommendations. A LangGraph fact-checker checks narrative claims against collected rankings and scores, but no human reviews reports before delivery; marketing staff decide whether to act afterward.

## 2. Risk classification
**First-pass tier: Minimal risk (provisional).** The stated marketing-analysis purpose does not appear to involve prohibited practices under **Article 5** or any high-risk area under **Article 6(2) and Annex III** of the EU AI Act. The input form and emailed analysis do not, on the facts provided, establish a direct AI-interaction disclosure duty under **Article 50(1)**. Whether **Article 50(2)** output-marking duties apply to the system's generated text requires further examination of the provider's technical role and applicable exceptions; internal use alone does not settle that question. Uncertainties include the final interface, possible external distribution, vendor data flows and actual marking capabilities.

## 3. Role map
| Role | Party / responsibilities to verify |
|---|---|
| **Provider** | The person or organisation placing the integrated agent on the market or putting it into service under its own name; confirm ownership and branding. Assess applicable AI Act duties, including **Article 4 AI literacy** and any relevant Article 50 obligations. |
| **Deployer** | The brand or SEO agency using the system; operate it for its intended marketing purpose, train users appropriately and govern reliance on outputs. |
| **Third-party vendors** | **OpenAI** (generation), **Serper** (search), **NewsAPI** (news), **Pinecone** (SEO-guideline retrieval), **Render** (fact-checker hosting), **QuickChart** (charts), **Gmail SMTP** (delivery), and **n8n** (workflow orchestration). Verify which services receive personal data and their contractual roles. |
| **GDPR roles** | Identify the controller(s) and processor(s) separately from AI Act provider/deployer labels; verify vendor agreements and any international-transfer safeguards. |

## 4. Compliance findings

**Finding 1 — Personal-data governance and retention**  
**Severity:** Significant  
**Description:** Recipient email addresses and incidental names in news/search results may be processed. n8n execution logging remains at default settings, with no written retention or access policy. Vendor arrangements, privacy information and transfers have not been verified.  
**Recommended action:** Document data flows and lawful basis; provide appropriate privacy information; review processor contracts and transfers; define retention, deletion and access controls.  
**Escalation needed?** **Yes** — privacy lead/DPO or legal adviser.

**Finding 2 — Verification gaps and unchecked delivery**  
**Severity:** Significant  
**Description:** The fact-checker compares generated claims with collected data and calculated scores, but cannot independently detect source-data or brand-matching errors. Recommendations receive less systematic checking. If the checker fails, the report is still emailed, labelled *unchecked*.  
**Recommended action:** Test source and brand matching, validate recommendations, and hold unchecked reports for review or obtain explicit recipient acceptance of the limitation before delivery.  
**Escalation needed?** **Yes** — engineering/product owner.

**Finding 3 — AI output transparency**  
**Severity:** Minor, pending legal assessment  
**Description:** Reports contain AI-generated narrative without an AI notice or machine-readable marking. A specific Article 50 breach cannot be established from the supplied evidence; Article 50(2) applicability should be verified rather than dismissed solely because reports are internal.  
**Recommended action:** Label the narrative as AI-generated, distinguish it from code-calculated scores, and assess whether technical output marking is required.  
**Escalation needed?** **Yes** — provider's compliance/legal contact.

**Finding 4 — Operating controls and evidence**  
**Severity:** Minor  
**Description:** Only the builder currently accesses n8n, but documented permissions, incident handling, test results and change-control arrangements were not supplied.  
**Recommended action:** Record access permissions, failed-API behaviour, quality tests, incident steps and an accountable maintainer.  
**Escalation needed?** **No** — address within project governance.

## 5. Overall recommendation
**Proceed with conditions.** No prohibited or Annex III high-risk purpose was identified for the described use. **Before production deployment**, document GDPR roles, lawful processing, vendor safeguards and retention; strengthen source validation and prevent unreviewed delivery when fact-checking fails; and assess Article 50 output-marking requirements. Add a clear AI-generation notice and retain evidence of testing. These conditions address material risks without treating the current system as a high-risk AI application.

## 6. What this report is not
This educational review is **not a legal opinion, conformity assessment or certification**. Conclusions and applicable obligations should be verified with qualified legal counsel before EU market placement or production deployment.

# Phase 5 — Debrief Conversation

## 1. Auditor presents

My audit classified the AI Search Visibility Agent as **minimal risk** for its stated internal SEO/GEO use, with a recommendation to **proceed with conditions**.

The four main findings were:

1. **GDPR governance and retention:** Undocumented retention arrangements and insufficiently defined privacy and vendor controls.
2. **Source verification:** Limitations in independently validating the original source data used by the agent.
3. **Unchecked report delivery:** Reports marked *unchecked* are still delivered when the fact-checker fails.
4. **AI transparency:** Missing AI-generated-content labelling and the need to assess potentially applicable transparency obligations.

I presented these findings to Barsha during our peer-audit debrief.

## 2. Builder responds

During our live discussion and subsequent written clarifications, Barsha explained that the reports are intended for internal marketing use; n8n uses default execution logging without a written retention policy; and LangGraph checks generated claims against retrieved rankings and calculated scores, but does not independently validate the original source data.

If the fact-checker is unavailable, the report is still delivered with an *unchecked* warning.

Barsha also shared the four gaps identified in her original self-audit:

1. **AI-generated-content labelling:** Neither the report nor the email clearly identifies the content as AI-generated. She flagged a possible Article 50(2) transparency duty for legal review.
2. **Reader-facing limitations:** Important limitations, including API-versus-ChatGPT differences, name-based matching and one-day snapshots, are documented in the README but not communicated directly to report readers.
3. **Misleading comparative claims:** Competitor comparisons could create misleading-advertising risks under German UWG rules if clients reuse the findings publicly without human review.
4. **GDPR governance:** No privacy notice, undefined retention, a personal Gmail sender, and potential personal-data transfers to US vendors.

Barsha clarified that she had already recognised the fact-checker's dependence on collected source data but had treated it as a limitation rather than a separate reliability risk.

She had also regarded the unchecked-report fallback as a robustness feature, whereas my independent audit highlighted the risk of distributing unverified findings.

We discussed these differences and the value of adding safeguards, including human review and stronger verification before report delivery.

### Findings we both identified

We agreed on GDPR governance and retention concerns, as well as the need for clearer AI transparency.

### Findings I identified that Barsha had not fully recognised as risks

**Unchecked report delivery:** The workflow continues sending reports when fact-checking fails. Barsha had originally treated this as a robustness feature, while my audit identified the potential risk of distributing unverified information.

**Source verification:** Barsha had documented the limitation, but my audit more explicitly assessed it as a reliability risk because the fact-checker cannot independently confirm the accuracy of the original source data.

### Findings Barsha identified that I had not separately flagged

**Misleading comparative claims:** Competitor comparisons could create misleading-advertising risks if the findings are reused publicly without adequate review.

**Reader-facing limitations:** Report recipients are not directly informed about important system limitations, even though these are documented in the README.

### Differences in interpretation and severity

**AI transparency:** Barsha raised Article 50(2) as a possible legal obligation, while I treated its applicability as requiring further assessment. We agreed that legal review and clear AI-generated-content labelling were appropriate.

**Human review:** Barsha proposed an optional review-before-send step for client reports. I focused on preventing unchecked reports from being delivered when verification fails. These recommendations complement each other.

**Overall severity:** Both assessments recommended **Proceed with conditions**, with no findings classified as blocking.
