# Clarifying Questions Log

## Question 1: Who places the system on the market under their name?

- **What I need to know:** Whether the software vendor, the trading company, or another integrator develops and supplies the final system under its own name.
- **Why it matters:** This determines who is the provider and who is the deployer under Article 3 of the EU AI Act.
- **Provisional assumption:** The software vendor is the provider and the trading company is the deployer.

## Question 2: Can the system select equipment, suppliers, or prices automatically?

- **What I need to know:** Whether the tool only drafts and flags discrepancies, or whether it can make or execute purchasing and safety-related decisions.
- **Why it matters:** The degree of autonomy affects the risk analysis and the strength of required human controls.
- **Provisional assumption:** The system only recommends and drafts. A qualified expert selects the supplier, confirms the price, and controls sending.

## Question 3: Can any proposal be sent without expert approval?

- **What I need to know:** Whether there are technical controls preventing automatic or accidental sending.
- **Why it matters:** Human review is the main control against unsupported claims, unsuitable equipment, and financial or safety harm.
- **Provisional assumption:** Sending is technically blocked until expert approval and resolution of essential discrepancies.

## Question 4: What information is sent to third-party AI or cloud providers?

- **What I need to know:** Whether customer inquiries, names, emails, prices, supplier documents, trade secrets, or personal data leave the company's environment, and what retention and transfer rules apply.
- **Why it matters:** This may trigger GDPR, confidentiality, trade-secret, security, and contractual requirements.
- **Provisional assumption:** Some documents may contain personal and confidential commercial information, but the brief provides no data-flow or vendor information.

## Question 5: How are generated claims, evidence links, and corrections recorded?

- **What I need to know:** Whether every important claim is linked to an approved source and whether reviews, overrides, unresolved discrepancies, and final approvals are logged.
- **Why it matters:** Traceability is necessary for reliable review, accountability, incident investigation, and quality control.
- **Provisional assumption:** Supporting references are shown to the expert, but systematic logging and testing have not been confirmed.
